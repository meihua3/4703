Design Fiction as Critical Method

This design fiction builds on insights from A2, which evaluated a Multilingual Announcement Feed designed to improve access for digital refugees and linguistically diverse communities. While the prototype enhanced inclusion through expanded language support, the evaluation revealed ongoing gaps in trust, governance, and systemic accountability. Users reported declining confidence even as technical features improved, pointing to tensions between usability metrics and lived experience.

A3 extends these insights by imagining a near-future where every public digital system must pass a mandatory Digital Fairness Audit before deployment. FairCity Check portrays a world where fairness evaluation itself becomes institutionalized policy, transforming ethical considerations into measurable scores and algorithmic certifications. This scenario explores how evaluation frameworks can shift from tools for improvement into gatekeeping mechanisms that define what counts as "fair."

The fiction raises subtle questions about accountability, automation, and authority in digital governance. When fairness is reduced to numerical thresholds (e.g., "comprehension ≥ 90%"), who decides these benchmarks? When algorithmic confidence reaches 0.94, should human oversight become optional? When translation accuracy shows 89% but users report confusion, which measure defines success? These tensions reflect broader challenges in HCI: the field often prioritizes efficiency and usability while inadequately addressing power dynamics, contextual justice, and long-term social impacts.

By presenting a plausible yet uncomfortable future, FairCity Check invites reflection on current trajectories in digital governance. It does not predict what will happen, but rather asks: if fairness audits become mandatory, what forms of inclusion might be legitimized—and what exclusions might be rendered invisible? The design fiction method allows exploration of these questions without prescribing solutions, creating space for critical dialogue about the values embedded in sociotechnical systems.
